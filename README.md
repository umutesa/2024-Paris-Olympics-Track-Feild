# 2024-Paris-Olympics-Track-Feild
In this project, we explored the 2024 Olympics through a focused lens—using SQL filtering to analyze performance trends in track and field events. The dataset, sourced from Kaggle, offers a limited but intriguing snapshot, capturing only track and field disciplines. While this means other sports are excluded, the insights gathered still tell a compelling story.

## Key Insights from the Data

### 1.Countries with the Highest Participation

Some nations clearly took center stage in terms of participation. The top five were:

- United States  
- Germany  
- Australia  
- France  
- United Kingdom  

Their consistent presence reflects their long-standing investment in athletics.

### 2. Most Popular Events

Participation numbers revealed a few standout events:

- 100 metres  
- 800 metres  
- Javelin throw  
- 1500 metres  
- 400 metres  
- 200 metres  

These races attracted the most athletes, perhaps due to their historic appeal or training accessibility.

### 3. Least Popular Events

These events had significantly fewer participants:

- Marathon Race Walk Mixed Relay  
- 4x1000 metres Relay  
- 10,000 metres  
- 4x400 metres Relay  
- Triple Jump  
- 20km Race Walk  

Their technical demands or lack of public attention may be contributing factors.

### 4. Time of Day and Athletic Performance

A surprising pattern emerged—performance seems to vary depending on the time of day.

- **Best performance times**: 07:30, 08:00, 09:50, 18:20, and 20:57  
- **Least optimal**: Between 10:50 AM and 12:50 PM  

This observation offers a compelling look into how environment and biology shape athletic performance. Cooler temperatures during early mornings and evenings may reduce heat stress, improving comfort and lowering the risk of dehydration. These conditions support better thermoregulation, helping athletes perform more efficiently. Performance also appears influenced by circadian rhythms—our internal clocks that affect energy, alertness, and physical strength. Early and late hours often align more closely with these natural peaks.
This has real implications. Event organizers might consider adjusting competition times to favor cooler parts of the day, aiming to enhance safety and improve performance. Similarly, athletes and coaches could revise training plans to focus on intense workouts during those more favorable windows. Athletic performance tends to dip between 10:50 AM and 12:50 PM due rising temperatures: These hours often coincide with increasing heat and sunlight exposure, leading to physical discomfort, faster dehydration, and decreased endurance. High temperatures can hinder muscle efficiency and overall stamina.

### 5. Gender Representation Across Events

Participation was fairly balanced, but dominance varied by event.

- **Male-dominated**: 5000 metres, 10,000 metres, marathon, and 110m hurdles  
- **Female-dominated**: 1500 metres, high jump, and 20km race walk  

This may reflect training preferences or cultural trends in sports development.

### 6. Top Athletes and Performance Trends

We also highlighted top performers, analyzing their placement (POS) and marks. While individual results vary, strong preparation and national backing were recurring patterns.

### 7. Predictive Modeling: Forecasting Future Outcomes

Using event-specific data, we trained a simple model to predict outcomes for new participants.  
Key variables: event type, athlete gender, and time of day.  
While accuracy is limited, the model offers a glimpse into what predictive sports analytics could become.
